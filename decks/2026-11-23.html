FDA clears AI that predicts 5-year breast cancer risk from mammograms

Evidence FDA 510(k) clearance · 65k-mammogram validation, no accuracy figures published
Where in careBreast screening · radiology
At the bedsideNow (US)
  • DeepHealth's Mammo Risk (510(k) K262709) estimates 3-, 4- and 5-year breast cancer risk from an existing 3D screening mammogram, with no questionnaire
  • Validated on 65,000+ screening mammograms from 10 US sites, with consistent performance reported in Black women and dense breasts; no accuracy numbers released
  • Not a cancer detector, no outcome data yet, and not the first: Clairity Breast got FDA De Novo for 5-year risk from 2D mammograms in May 2025
Why it matters for clinicians

Image-based risk could replace questionnaire models like Tyrer-Cuzick when deciding who needs supplemental MRI or shorter screening intervals.

AI-designed drugs are genuinely outperforming in Phase II

Evidence Consultancy analysis · 46 programs, not peer-reviewed
Where in careDrug development · Phase II trials
At the bedside5+ years to approved drugs
  • Oliver Wyman analysis: AI-native drug developers progressed 45% of Phase II programs successfully, vs 29% for the broader sector (46-program sample)
  • AI drug discovery investment and licensing deals hit $46 billion in H1 2026 — after $43B in 2025 and just $12B in 2024

GPT-6.2 Astra ships to harsh reviews — but tops the med-chemistry benchmark

Evidence Vendor benchmark · 99 test items
Where in careResearch · drug discovery
At the bedsideNot clinical yet
  • OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on Nov 17; early reception was sharply negative — one prominent tester called it the 'worst model OpenAI has ever released'
  • Yet on MedChemBench (a 99-item medicinal-chemistry task set) Astra earned 84.5% — a leading score, though the benchmark itself cautions it does not prove real drug-discovery capability
  • Google answered Nov 18 with Gemini 4 Neon at $3/$15 per million tokens and a one-million-token context window — the price war continues
Why it matters for clinicians

Frontier labs now ship medical-science benchmarks alongside each release — a clear signal of where they think the value is heading.

Decoder

This week's terms, in plain language

Image-based risk model
AI that reads a normal-looking image for subtle patterns linked to future disease, not for cancer present today. The output is a risk estimate, like a Tyrer-Cuzick score, not a finding.
De Novo authorization
FDA route for a new kind of device with nothing equivalent on the market. Requires more evidence than a 510(k), which only needs equivalence to an existing device.
AI-designed drug
An ordinary medicine (pill or biologic) where machine-learning models helped choose the target or design the molecule. It passes the same trials and approval as any drug: 'AI' describes how it was found, not how it works.
Phase II trial
The first trial testing whether a drug actually works in patients with the condition (typically ~100–300), after Phase I safety testing. Historically the stage where most candidates fail.
Benchmark
A fixed test set used to score AI models, like a board exam. A high score shows the model answers those questions well, not that it performs safely in real practice.
Tokens & context window
A token is ~¾ of a word; models are priced per million tokens. The context window is how much the model can read at once: 1M tokens ≈ 750,000 words, enough for many complete patient records.
510(k) clearance
The most common FDA route for devices: the maker shows the product is 'substantially equivalent' to one already on the market. Usually requires less clinical evidence than full approval (PMA).
Triage AI
Software that flags and reprioritises cases (e.g. a suspected bleed on CT) so a clinician sees them sooner. The clinician still reads the case and makes the diagnosis.
IPO
Initial public offering: a private company sells shares on a stock exchange for the first time. It raises cash and requires public, audited reporting, so outsiders learn far more about how the company works and earns money.
